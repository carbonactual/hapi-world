# MARKET UNIVERSE CANON — The Canonical Law of Markets & Economic Objects
**Carbon Actual Ecosystem — Canonical Layer (hapi-world/canon/)**
**Ratified: September 16, 2026 · Status: Law (Canon) · Siblings: TRADE_CANON.md, OMNI_BROWSER.md, VOICE_VIDEO_SURFACE_LAW.md**

> **Position:** This is the law of *what a market object is*. TIP — and any product that deals in markets, exchange, investment or value — **taps** this canon. Products instantiate markets of these canonical types; they never invent new market primitives, and they never redefine this file. TRADE_CANON.md governs *how trade flows*; this canon governs *what can be traded and represented*.

---

## PART 0 — THE DEEPEST PRIMITIVE (the governing definition)

The architectural definition changes from "TIP trades assets" to:

**TIP is an interoperable market infrastructure for discovering, representing, verifying, financing, contracting, exchanging, settling and managing economic value in the form of assets, rights, claims, obligations, opportunities, capabilities, capacities, information and relationships.**

This accommodates the old economy, current financial markets, digital assets, environmental markets, the emerging agent economy and future markets without repeatedly inventing a new primitive.

**The governing design principle (loophole-proofing at the deepest level):**

*Do not create a new market primitive when an existing economic object, right, claim, contract, evidence model or settlement mechanism can express it. Create a new primitive only when the economic semantics genuinely differ.*

This is the key protection against the ecosystem becoming a pile of disconnected "features."

## PART I — THE MARKET OBJECT (canonical representation)

A market object is not merely an "asset." The canonical market object must be able to represent:

**Asset + Right + Claim + Obligation + Opportunity + Capability + Capacity + Evidence + Relationship**

That allows the ecosystem to handle conventional securities alongside things conventional exchanges leave outside their perimeter.

## PART II — CORE MARKET-OBJECT FAMILIES

### II-1. Financial value
Shares and equities; bonds and notes; funds and fund interests; private-company shares; convertible instruments; SAFEs and venture instruments; receivables; invoices; royalties; revenue shares; loans; credit; trade finance; structured products; derivatives; options; futures; forwards; swaps; CFDs where legally permitted; insurance-linked securities; catastrophe/longevity/cyber-risk instruments.

Private secondary markets already demonstrate that privately held securities can be a distinct market layer rather than being restricted to public exchanges.

### II-2. Currencies and monetary instruments
Fiat; FX; stablecoins; tokenised deposits; central-bank settlement assets where accessible; e-money/stored value; gift cards; vouchers; airtime; data bundles; loyalty points; game currencies; commercial credits; mutual-credit balances; local/community currencies.

**Canonical rule:** the ecosystem supports *multiple settlement assets*, not just "crypto." BIS's 2026 work distinguishes stablecoins, tokenised deposits and central-bank settlement; Project Agorá demonstrates atomic multi-currency settlement as an emerging infrastructure pattern.

### II-3. Physical commodities
Precious metals; base metals; critical minerals; solid minerals; oil; gas; agricultural commodities; livestock; fisheries; timber; water; salt; biomass; industrial feedstocks; animal by-products; recyclables; scrap; waste streams; secondary raw materials.

### II-4. Stored and document-backed commodities
First-class support for the chain:

**Warehouse Receipt → Inventory → Collateral → Trade → Settlement**

The 2024 UNCITRAL/UNIDROIT Model Law treats warehouse receipts as instruments capable of transferring goods and supporting collateralisation; Nigeria's SEC has established rules around commodity exchanges, electronic warehouse receipts, warehouse operators and collateral management.

Canonical document objects: warehouse receipts; electronic warehouse receipts; bills of lading; electronic bills of lading; bills of exchange; promissory notes; electronic transferable records; certificates of origin; inspection certificates; customs documents; insurance documents; title documents. UNCITRAL's electronic-transferable-record framework expressly covers bills of lading, bills of exchange, promissory notes and warehouse receipts.

## PART III — THE RIGHTS MARKET

One of the largest gaps in conventional architecture. The ecosystem must distinguish:

**ownership ≠ possession ≠ custody ≠ control ≠ use ≠ access ≠ license ≠ entitlement ≠ beneficial interest**

Canonical rights types: ownership rights; beneficial ownership; legal title; possession; custody; usage rights; access rights; subscription rights; membership rights; voting rights; governance rights; franchise rights; distribution rights; territory rights; exclusivity; non-exclusivity; licensing; sub-licensing; assignment; easements; concessions; extraction rights; mineral rights; water rights; airspace rights; spectrum rights; transit rights; landing/slot rights; capacity rights; reservation rights; booking rights; naming rights; sponsorship rights; media rights; broadcast rights; streaming rights; publication rights; royalty rights.

WIPO already treats technology/IP as transferable economic objects through assignments, licensing and other commercial arrangements.

## PART IV — THE IDENTITY ECONOMY

Identity-linked identifiers form a market family: domains; subdomains; usernames; handles; vanity names; phone numbers; short codes; merchant IDs; account aliases; organisation identifiers; namespace identifiers; brand names; digital certificates; verifiable credentials; DID-related identifiers; service identities; agent identities.

**Canonical constraint — identifiers are not presumed property.** Every identifier market object must carry:

**Identifier → Control model → Legal status → Transferability → Restrictions → Custodian → Expiry**

That prevents the dangerous mistake of treating a merely technical identifier as property.

## PART V — THE OPPORTUNITY MARKET (market-discovery, not just order books)

The ecosystem must allow someone to say: *"I found something that does not yet have a conventional market."*

Canonical opportunity examples: a company someone wants to acquire; a mine needing capital; a factory with unused capacity; a warehouse with empty space; a truck returning empty; a cargo needing transport; a buyer looking for an unavailable commodity; a seller looking for financing; a patent looking for a commercial partner; a startup looking for investors; a technology looking for a licensee; a carbon project seeking offtake; a recycling stream looking for a processor; an agricultural harvest looking for forward buyers; a business needing working capital; a local infrastructure project needing pooled finance; a market niche nobody has served; a missing supplier; a missing distributor; a missing capability; a missing connection; a missing network; a missing technology; a problem somebody will pay to solve.

**Canonical opportunity lifecycle:**

Opportunity → discovery → verification → qualification → expression of interest → matching → offer → financing → contract → execution → settlement → outcome → feedback/value measurement.

This makes TIP a *market-discovery engine*, not merely an order book.

## PART VII — ENVIRONMENTAL AND PLANETARY MARKETS (not reducible to "carbon")

**Climate:** carbon allowances; carbon credits; carbon offsets; carbon removals; carbon avoidance; methane reductions; soil carbon; carbon farming; enhanced rock weathering; biochar; DAC; BioCCS; BECCS; reforestation; afforestation; peatland restoration; carbon offtakes; carbon pre-purchases; carbon futures/forwards where permitted.

The EU's 2026 certification methodologies cover DACCS, BioCCS, biochar, agricultural/agroforestry, peatland restoration and afforestation.

**Beyond carbon:** biodiversity credits; nature credits; habitat credits; water-quality credits; water restoration; nutrient credits; wetland credits; soil-health attributes; ecosystem-service claims; renewable-energy certificates; energy attributes; circularity attributes; recycled-content certificates.

**Canonical rule:** the system must be *extensible* so future environmental instruments do not require a new core primitive.

## PART VIII — THE CIRCULAR ECONOMY AS AN ACTUAL MARKET

## PART XIV — OLD MECHANISMS REVIVED (not rebuilt from scratch, resurrected digitally)

Canonical revived mechanisms: barter; mutual credit; bills of exchange; promissory notes; warehouse receipts; bills of lading; consignment; factoring; forfaiting; escrow; letters of credit; collateralised trade; auction markets; forward contracts; options; futures; clearing; netting; commodity pooling; cooperatives; buying clubs; investment clubs; syndicates; guild-like networks; local/community currencies; reciprocal purchasing; time/capacity exchange.

The goal is not to romanticise older systems — it is to recover economically useful mechanisms whose interfaces were constrained by paper, geography and fragmented ledgers. UNCITRAL's electronic-transferable-record work embodies this principle: legally meaningful paper instruments recreated electronically without losing commercial function.

## PART XV — MODERN AGENTIC COMMERCE

Canonical agent-commerce chain:

Human/Organisation → delegates authority → Agent receives bounded mandate → discovers market → evaluates offers → negotiates → contracts → pays → receives → reports.

With mandatory controls: policy → spending limit → asset class permission → counterparty rules → jurisdiction → risk limit → approval threshold → audit trail.

UNCITRAL's 2024 Model Law on Automated Contracting addresses legal recognition of automated and AI-assisted contracting. (This part binds ABBA's orchestration of all agents — per the ABBA Master Capability Registry and SealGrant governance.)

## PART XVI — TRADE-DOCUMENT INFRASTRUCTURE

The ecosystem trades not merely things but *the documents that control or evidence things*: eBL; warehouse receipts; electronic transferable records; invoices; purchase orders; certificates of origin; inspection certificates; customs declarations; export permits; import permits; sanitary certificates; phytosanitary certificates; insurance certificates; ATA Carnets; proof of delivery; proof of performance; proof of custody.

ICC reports eBL interoperability as a major issue; FIATA standardises interoperable electronic freight documentation; the IMO's 2026 maritime electronic-business data compendium improves ship-port-authority interoperability.

## PART XVII — THE SETTLEMENT GRAPH

More than "Buy" and "Sell." Canonical settlement methods: Delivery-versus-Payment; Payment-versus-Payment; Delivery-versus-Delivery; atomic swap; escrow; conditional release; milestone release; streaming payment; installment payment; net settlement; multi-party waterfall; revenue waterfall; collateral lock; margin; variation margin; repo; repurchase; securities lending; borrowing; leasing; rent-to-own; subscription; deferred settlement.

Nigeria's market has moved to T+1 for eligible equities and commodities, with DvP explicitly maintained at settlement.

**Canonical rule:** *Every transaction must specify its settlement method and settlement-finality model.*

## PART XVIII — THE MARKET MECHANICS ENGINE

Multiple market mechanisms, not one generic order book. Canonical mechanisms: fixed-price sale; negotiated sale; RFQ; RFP; auction; reverse auction; Dutch auction; English auction; sealed bid; continuous order book; OTC; peer-to-peer; brokered; dealer market; AMM; swap; pool; syndication; crowdfunding; subscription; tender; matching; batch auction; procurement; competitive allocation; allocation by eligibility; allocation by priority; lottery where legally appropriate; portfolio construction.

## PART XIX — EVIDENCE BEFORE VALUE (loophole resistance)

Every market object carries an evidence graph:

**Claim → Source → Evidence → Verification → Attestation → Confidence → Challenge → Resolution**

Canonical evidence queries: "Seller owns this gold." "This company owns this patent." "This warehouse contains 100 tonnes." "This carbon removal actually occurred." "This account controls this identifier." "This factory has 10 MW spare capacity." "This invoice has not already been financed." "This company really generated this revenue."

Evidence sources: registries; government records; oracles; IoT; satellite data; auditors; inspectors; custodians; banks; exchanges; blockchains; corporate registries; product passports; human attestations; AI verification.

## PART XX — THE ANTI-LOOPHOLE LAYER (unusually strong architecture)

**Prevent:** double ownership (one asset cannot be sold twice); double financing (one invoice/warehouse receipt cannot be financed beyond permitted claims); double spending (a transferable digital claim cannot be consumed in two incompatible settlements); double counting (especially carbon, environmental attributes, inventory — the EU's carbon-removal certification rules require registries preventing double counting with lifecycle traceability).

**Track:** encumbrance; pledge; lien; mortgage; lease; reservation; lock; freeze; court order; regulatory restriction; transfer restriction; sanctions status; beneficial ownership; custody; possession; jurisdiction; tax status; expiry; revocation; cancellation; retirement; consumption; redemption.

## PART XXI — THE MARKET-OBJECT LIFECYCLE (canonical state machine)

Discovered → Claimed → Identified → Verified → Qualified → Listed → Priced → Offered → Matched → Contracted → Financed → Reserved → Transferred → Settled → Held → Used → Transformed → Resold → Retired → Expired → Destroyed → Recycled → Reissued (where legally permissible).

This allows the ecosystem to follow an asset for its *entire economic life*, not only the moment it is traded.

## PART XXII — PROVENANCE AND TRANSFORMATION (asset lineage as first-class primitive)

A kilogram of ore becomes metal; metal becomes machinery; machinery produces goods; goods become waste; waste becomes recovered material; recovered material becomes another product.

**Asset lineage is a first-class primitive:**

Mine → ore → shipment → warehouse → processor → refined metal → manufacturer → component → product → resale → recycling → recovered material.

The EU Digital Product Passport direction reinforces lifecycle traceability and machine-readable product identity.

## PART XXIII — CROSS-ASSET COMPOSITION

One market object may represent a composite:
- **Company** = shares + debt + IP + property + contracts + inventory + customers + liabilities + licences + future cash flow.
- **Carbon project** = land rights + technology + project company + carbon-removal capacity + future credits + offtake + financing + environmental attributes.
- **Mine** = concession + mineral resource + equipment + production capacity + transport access + offtake + royalty + financing.

Canonical types: **Composite Asset / Composite Opportunity**, with subordinate claims and relationships.

## PART XXIV — MARKETPLACE OF MARKETS (interoperability over ownership)

The ecosystem must not insist on owning every market. It discovers and connects existing markets: exchange; broker; bank; custodian; commodity market; carbon registry; digital-asset venue; private market; auction house; government registry; OTC counterparty; local marketplace.

The 2026 trade ecosystem suffers from too many standards that do not fit together; ICC's Digital Trade Navigator was created to navigate the growing collection of digital-trade standards.

**Canonical role: universal orchestration/interoperability layer — not necessarily the owner of every underlying market.**

## PART XXV — JURISDICTION AWARENESS (Nigeria adapters built-in, not bolted-on)

Nigeria's 2026 regulatory direction explicitly covers: Digital Assets; Digital Asset Exchanges; Digital Asset Custody; RWA Tokenisation and Offering Platforms; Commodity Markets; Warehousing; Collateral Management; Commodity Brokers/Dealers (SEC Nigeria's published capital requirements distinguish these categories). Proposed rules cover online FX and CFD services, including platform providers and offshore entities targeting Nigerian residents. CBN's Payments System Vision 2028 centres interoperability, security, inclusion, innovation and cross-border integration. PAPSS is expanding instant cross-border local-currency payment connectivity in Africa.

**Canonical rule: the ecosystem is jurisdiction-aware, never jurisdiction-blind.**

## PART XXVII — TAP RULES

1. Products **inherit** this canon. TIP is the first instantiation; OMNI, SPOTIST, Open Bank and future products may tap the same market universe for their own domains.
2. Products may add *instances* of canonical types; they may not invent new primitives where canonical semantics suffice (Part 0 design principle).
3. Every market object must carry its evidence graph (Part XIX) and lifecycle (Part XXI). No evidence, no value.
4. The anti-loophole layer (Part XX) is non-negotiable in every implementation.
5. Amendment rights rest solely with the constitutional layer (CANON.md custodianship).

**Future direction noted as canonical insight:** markets for unused capacity and unrealised value — electricity flexibility, carbon-removal offtakes, data access, private-company secondary transactions, technology licensing and digital product passports all point to the same architecture: *identify something that exists, something that can be produced, or something that is currently unused — then make its economic potential legible and transactable.*
