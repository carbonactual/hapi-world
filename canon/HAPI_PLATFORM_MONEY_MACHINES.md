## PART 1 — WHY PLATFORMS SHARE REVENUE AT ALL (the supply-cost law)

Platforms sell one thing: attention inventory. Ads, commerce GMV, subscriptions — all of it converts attention into money. Creators are not the platform's customers — **creators are the unpaid-to-underpaid supply chain that manufactures the inventory.**

Revenue sharing exists for exactly three reasons:
1. **Supply security.** Content keeps users inside the app. The share is the cost of keeping the factory running and exclusive.
2. **Retention war economics.** Payouts rise when platforms compete for the same creators (2021-2025 creator fund wars). Payouts fall when a platform has no competitor (TikTok's near-monopoly on short-form = the lowest per-view rates in the industry).
3. **Commerce seeding.** Affiliate payouts are cheaper than ads: TikTok Shop GMV is ~42% affiliate-driven [VERIFIED] — creators sell for free until they sell.

The law that falls out: **a platform pays creators in proportion to how much of its own revenue the creator's content directly generates, minus its monopoly power.** YouTube's long-form video = premium CPM inventory → 55% share, the industry's highest. TikTok's short-form = cheap ads → $0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views [VERIFIED]. WhatsApp pays creators nothing — because it monetizes BUSINESSES (API fees), not attention. That is why WhatsApp is a settlement rail, not an earning platform: value settles there, it is not minted there.

## PART 6 — MINTING, TOKENIZATION, OWNERSHIP (the white space that closes the loop)

Here is the punchline of the entire money map: **no platform sells ownership.** Every payout on every platform is rent for inventory you never own — followers, reach, even "your" storefront lives on their books. The platforms pay the minimum that keeps supply flowing while keeping the assets (the relationship graph, the payment rail, the identity) on their own balance sheets. The inversion doc predicted this; the money map confirms it: payouts are the supply-cost knob platforms tune.

What does not exist yet anywhere — and therefore the HAPi position:
1. **Portable identity/ownership instruments** (the # namespace, Proof chains) that a creator carries across platforms — your verified trust rank, not Meta's checkmark
2. **ContentLicense as the ownership ledger** (already live in the ecosystem DB) — licensing, exclusivity, territory, share, consent proof, mint reference; the permission law operationalized
3. **Fractionalized works** — community finances the next album/course; the bank settles (claims, escrow, netting — Trade Canon machinery)
4. **Archive-as-asset (RITES)** — the content estate that pays while the creator rests
5. **Value-used rating** — the metric class platforms cannot issue because their books need pulse, not value

A platform can copy a payout rate overnight; none of them can copy ownership — their entire business model is renting you what you make. That is the moat, and it is constitutional.
