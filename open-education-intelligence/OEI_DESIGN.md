# Open Education Intelligence (OEI)

**Status:** Canonical design
**Position:** InstituteGPT → Education System → Open Education Intelligence → Open Student Environment → Institutional Environments
**First institutional specialization:** NOUN

## 1. Purpose

Open Education Intelligence (OEI) is the universal intelligence and coordination layer for open, distance, online, blended, campus-supported, vocational, professional and lifelong learning.

OEI is not a university, not an LMS, not merely an OER repository, and not merely a chatbot. It is an education intelligence graph with interfaces and institutional adapters.

Its purpose is to understand and connect:

- learners and their goals;
- institutions and their authoritative rules/services;
- educators, tutors, mentors and communities;
- curricula, courses and learning pathways;
- knowledge, OER and learning media;
- practice and assessment;
- research and projects;
- services and support;
- credentials and evidence;
- skills and portfolios;
- careers, work and enterprise;
- scholarships, grants and other legitimate opportunity information;
- student life and communities;
- institutional operations and quality;
- relationships, events, authority, consent, provenance and outcomes.

## 2. Architectural Principle

The learner is the primary participant. An institution is a relationship and authority context around the learner, not the definition of the learner.

A person's education identity must persist across institutional and learning transitions.

Example:

`prospective learner → admitted learner → student → researcher → graduate → worker/entrepreneur → lifelong learner`

The same human relationship can accumulate multiple institutional, programme, qualification, skill, project, work and community relationships over time.

## 4. Open Student Environment — Universal Scope

- lectures;

- video;
- simulations;
- tutorials;
- examples;

- external courses;
- resource collections.

Every resource should carry provenance and, where possible, licence/usage metadata.

Open does not mean unverified, unrestricted or unlicensed. OEI must distinguish access, licensing, institutional authority, quality and reuse permissions.

### 4.5 How-To & Microlearning

- how-to videos;
- tutorials;
- study skills;
- digital literacy;
- research skills;

### 4.6 Practice & Assessment Preparation

### 4.7 Assessment Integrity

OEI may explain rules, teach concepts, generate practice and help learners prepare.

OEI must not impersonate learners, sit examinations for them, complete graded work on their behalf, bypass identity/eligibility controls or perform unauthorized institutional submissions.

### 4.8 Research & Projects

For every connected institution or authorized service provider, OEI should model:

`request type → eligibility → required information → required documents → signatures/authority → deadline → submission route → status → evidence → next step`

Forms and institutional requests are first-class objects, not just links.

## 7. Institutional Adapter Model

Institution-specific implementation must not redefine the universal learner model.

An institutional adapter supplies:

1. authoritative institution identity;
2. institution policies/rules;
3. programme and curriculum data;
4. course/catalogue data;
5. calendars and deadlines;
6. institutional people/roles where permitted;
7. forms and request workflows;
8. services;
9. study-centre/campus information;
10. assessment/examination rules;
11. research/project workflows;
12. library resources;
13. support channels;
14. student-life information;
15. institutional opportunities;
16. graduation/credential workflows;
17. verification endpoints;
18. source provenance and freshness.

The adapter is the authority boundary for institution-specific facts.

## 9. Intelligence Functions

OEI intelligence operates through:

`Observe → Verify → Understand → Classify → Relate → Personalize → Recommend → Match → Notify → Escalate → Learn`

Examples:

- detect an approaching institutional deadline;
- identify missing requirements;
- find relevant OER;
- recommend practice for weak learning areas;
- connect a skill to a legitimate opportunity;
- find a suitable project/research path;
- match a student need with a verified service;
- route a complaint to the correct authority;
- identify when human intervention is needed.

## 10. Provenance & Trust

Every important education fact should be traceable to a source.

Source classes:

- institutional official source;
- regulator/government source;
- recognized education provider;
- library/research source;
- openly licensed resource;
- verified third-party source;
- community/user-generated source.

Each source should carry where practical:

`source_uri • publisher • authority • captured_at • last_verified • freshness • licence • evidence_type • confidence`

Official institutional facts outrank third-party summaries for institutional procedures.

## 11. Open Education Principles

OEI follows these principles:

- learner-centered;
- open by default where legally and ethically appropriate;
- evidence-backed;
- provenance-aware;
- licence-aware;
- inclusive and accessible;
- institution-neutral at the universal layer;
- institution-authoritative at the adapter layer;
- lifelong;
- interoperable;
- modular;
- relationship-based;
- consent-first;
- assessment-integrity preserving;
- human-authorized for consequential actions.

## 12. Authority Boundary

OEI may autonomously:

`read • analyze • classify • organize • monitor • recommend • explain • match • notify • report • escalate`

Institutional and consequential actions require appropriate authority and/or user approval.

The system must not:

- impersonate a learner;
- sit an exam for a learner;
- complete assessed work as the learner;
- bypass institutional identity or eligibility controls;
- expose private student data to marketplace participants without deliberate authorization;
- make regulated financial decisions/executions autonomously;
- move money or execute investments;
- publish institutional actions without authorization.

## 13. Interoperability Direction

OEI should be designed to connect to existing education systems rather than replace all of them.

Potential interfaces include:

`LMS/VLE • SIS • library systems • OER repositories • content repositories • assessment systems • credential systems • institutional portals • messaging • calendar • APIs • identity providers • research repositories • career systems • marketplace services`

Integration should be adapter-based and source-aware.

## 15. Research Basis

The design is informed by current education and ODL practice:

- UNESCO's 2019 OER Recommendation defines OER around openly licensed learning, teaching and research materials and organizes action around capacity building, policy, inclusive quality, sustainability and international cooperation. UNESCO updated its OER mandate page in May 2026.
- Nigeria's NUC currently separates Open and Distance Learning, Trans-National Education, e-Learning/content and learning infrastructure, and OER repositories/resource attribution in its Directorate structure.
- NUC's current e-learning guidelines identify OER, MOOCs, LMS/VLEs, virtual laboratories and collaborative platforms as part of the modern Nigerian higher-education ecosystem.
- The Commonwealth of Learning's 2026 learner-support work emphasizes that access alone is insufficient and highlights academic, psychological, digital and institutional support needs in ODL.
- The Open University demonstrates integrated distance-learning support spanning structured learning, tutor support, library access, computing help, career services, study skills, accessibility and student communities.
- Harvard Library's research-support model demonstrates the value of research-question development, literature search, evidence synthesis and subject/research support.

## 17. Definition to Preserve

> **Open Education Intelligence is the universal intelligence and coordination layer for people, institutions, educators, knowledge, learning, assessment, research, credentials, skills, services, opportunities and lifelong educational relationships across open, distance, online, blended and other learning environments.**

> **The Open Student Environment is the learner-facing ecosystem within OEI. Institutional environments such as NOUN are specialized, authoritative branches of that universal environment.**
